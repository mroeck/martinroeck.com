<span class="name">Dr. Martin Röck</span> — I am a researcher working on the
climate and environmental impacts of the built environment, seeking ways to support the emergence of regenerative systems of spatial production and use. My work focuses on
measuring and reducing the emissions caused by how we build — not only the energy a building uses once it stands, but the
carbon locked into its materials and construction long before anyone moves in
(for more, see [Research]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }})).

I am Founding Director and Lead Scientist of
[RISE Institute for Regenerative Spatial Systems Science](http://www.riselabs.at)
and of the [Global Building Data Initiative (GBDI)](https://www.gbdi.io), implemented together with
EPFL and a global network of partners including GLOBE, the Global Consensus on
Sustainability in the Built Environment.

Much of my work sits at the interface between science and policy. I advise and support the
European Commission, the International Energy Agency and the United Nations
Environment Programme on whole life carbon measurement and on what can actually
reduce emissions in practice. I currently serve as Lead Advisor on Embodied
Carbon for the UNEP GlobalABC Global Status Report for Buildings and
Construction, and contribute to the forthcoming Assessment Report of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, IPCC.

I hold a dual PhD in Architectural Engineering (KU Leuven) and Civil
Engineering Science (TU Graz), completed as a DOC Fellow of the Austrian
Academy of Sciences. Before that I trained and worked as an architect.

Outside the office I love the outdoors, cycle, climb and fly.
I co-founded [wohnlabor](https://www.wohnlabor.at/), an association
for participatory housing, and of several coalitions working on building
sector decarbonisation, serve on the boards of [Habitat2030](https://habitat2030.at/) and [Wohnprojekt Wien](https://wohnprojekt.wien/), and are a founding member of the [Low Carbon Construction Association](https://www.lowcarbon.construction/).
