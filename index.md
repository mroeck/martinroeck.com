<span class="name">Dr. Martin Röck</span> — I am a researcher working on the
climate and environmental impacts of the built environment. My work focuses on
measuring and reducing the emissions caused by how we build — not only the energy a building uses once it stands, but the
carbon locked into its materials and construction long before anyone moves in
(for more, see [Research]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }})).

I am Founding Director and Lead Scientist of
[RISE — Institute for Regenerative Spatial Systems Science](https://riseregenerate.com)
in Vienna, and Global Director and Lead Scientist of the
[Global Building Data Initiative (GBDI)](https://www.gbdi.io), implemented at
EPFL in Lausanne under the umbrella of GLOBE, the Global Consensus on
Sustainability in the Built Environment.

Much of my work sits at the interface between science and policy. I advise the
European Commission, the International Energy Agency and the United Nations
Environment Programme on whole life carbon measurement and on what actually
reduces emissions in practice. I currently serve as Lead Advisor on Embodied
Carbon for the UNEP GlobalABC Global Status Report for Buildings and
Construction, and chair the expert advisory group of the GLOBE Embodied Carbon
Benchmarking Taskforce.

I hold a dual PhD in Architectural Engineering (KU Leuven) and Civil
Engineering Science (TU Graz), completed as a DOC Fellow of the Austrian
Academy of Sciences. Before that I trained and worked as an architect.

Outside the office I fly paragliders, cycle, climb and go to the mountains.
I was a co-founder of [wohnlabor](https://www.wohnlabor.at/), an association
for participatory housing, and of several coalitions working on building
sector decarbonisation, including [Habitat2030](https://habitat2030.at/) and
the [Low Carbon Construction Association](https://www.lowcarbon.construction/).
